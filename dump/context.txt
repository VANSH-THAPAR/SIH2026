Finalized Project Context — SIH 2026 | PS 26165
1. Project Identity
Problem Statement: PS 26165
 Organization: Oil India Limited (OIL)
 Core Domain: HSE/HSSE Safety Intelligence
 Solution: AI/NLP Engine to Detect Serious Injury & Fatality (SIF) Precursors in OIL's Unsafe-Act/Unsafe-Condition and Near-Miss Reports
Core objective
Transform OIL's large volume of unstructured safety reports into proactive, explainable SIF precursor intelligence.
The system should not merely classify incidents. It should understand:
What happened → What could have happened → Which barrier failed → Which safety rule applies → Has it happened before → Is a dangerous pattern emerging → What should HSE prioritize?

2. Final Solution Concept
The proposed system is an AI-powered SIF Precursor Intelligence Platform that converts employee-written safety narratives into structured safety information, evaluates their potential for serious/fatal outcomes, identifies failed safety barriers, maps applicable IOGP Life-Saving Rules, compares events with OIL's historical safety memory, detects recurring/emerging patterns, and generates an explainable risk/priority assessment.

3. The Four Core Solution Pillars
These are the final four points to use when presenting the solution concisely.
1. AI-Powered Safety Narrative Intelligence
Convert OIL's unstructured Unsafe Act, Unsafe Condition, Near-Miss and Incident narratives into a standardized Safety Event Profile.
The system identifies:

The system uses contextual AI/NLP, semantic understanding and safety ontologies, rather than relying only on exact keywords. Therefore, different employee descriptions of the same underlying hazard can be normalized into the same safety concepts.

2. SIF Potential & Critical Barrier Intelligence
Assess an incident according to its potential outcome rather than only its actual outcome.
The system evaluates whether the event could realistically escalate to a fatality or serious/life-changing outcome, using factors such as:
Potential consequence
Hazard/energy involved
Personnel exposure
Critical barrier condition
Credible escalation pathway
It simultaneously identifies the applicable IOGP Life-Saving Rule and analyzes whether critical safety barriers were:
Effective / Partially Effective / Failed / Missing / Bypassed / Degraded / Unknown
The output is an explainable SIF Potential Score, supported by evidence and an escalation scenario.

3. OIL Safety Memory & Emerging Pattern Intelligence
Create a semantic Safety Memory from OIL's historical incident data.
Historical reports are converted into embeddings and stored in a vector-enabled database. New incidents are compared against historical events to identify:
Similar incidents
Recurring hazards
Repeated activities
Common barrier failures
Repeated Life-Saving Rule associations
Location/facility patterns
Time-based trends
The system goes beyond individual similarity and detects emerging combinations of weaknesses, such as:
Confined-space activity + repeated gas-testing failures + increasing recurrence.
This turns historical reports into an organizational learning system.

4. SIF Escalation Graph & Proactive Intervention Engine — Key Differentiator
Instead of stopping at:
“SIF = HIGH”
the system reconstructs how the precursor could escalate into a serious/fatal event.
Example:
Hazard
  ↓
Exposure
  ↓
Barrier Failure
  ↓
Escalation
  ↓
Potential Serious/Fatal Consequence
It also performs a “What-If?” / counterfactual analysis:
What additional failure or changed circumstance could have caused the near-miss to become a fatal event?
The system combines SIF potential, potential consequence, exposure, barrier health and historical recurrence to prioritize preventive intervention.

6. SIF Detection — Finalized Concept
This is one of the most important technical decisions.
SIF is NOT determined by:
Actual injury severity alone
For example:
A worker escapes a dangerous situation without injury.
Actual consequence:
No injury
But the event could still have had:
Fatality potential
Therefore:
Actual Outcome ≠ Potential Outcome
The SIF engine should primarily ask:
Could the event, under realistic circumstances, have resulted in a fatality or serious/life-changing outcome?
SIF evidence dimensions
Potential Consequence
        +
Hazard / Energy
        +
Exposure
        +
Critical Barrier Condition
        +
Credible Escalation Path
        ↓
SIF Potential
The system should return:
SIF Potential Score
SIF Classification
Confidence
Evidence
Potential Consequence
Escalation Scenario
Important distinction
The SIF Potential Score and Recurrence Score must remain separate.
A frequent event does not automatically become SIF.
SIF Potential
=
Potential seriousness of this event
while:
Recurrence
=
How repeatedly this weakness appears in historical data
They can subsequently contribute to an overall risk/priority assessment.

7. SIF Score vs "SIF Percentage"
For the prototype, use:
SIF Potential Score
rather than claiming:
"91% probability of SIF."
For example:
SIF Potential Score: 91/100
Classification: HIGH
Confidence: 94%
The 91/100 represents the strength of evidence for serious/fatal potential under the defined assessment framework, not a statistical probability that someone will die.
The scoring weights and thresholds should ultimately be validated against OIL's internal HSSE/risk methodology and domain experts rather than presented as official OIL/IOGP thresholds.

11. Barrier Failure Analysis
The barrier model should be structured.
{
  "barrier": "Atmospheric Gas Testing",
  "expected_state": "Gas test performed before entry",
  "observed_state": "Gas test not performed",
  "status": "FAILED",
  "failure_mode": "Not implemented",
  "evidence": "without gas testing"
}
Recommended barrier statuses:
EFFECTIVE
PARTIALLY_EFFECTIVE
FAILED
MISSING
BYPASSED
DEGRADED
UNKNOWN
NOT_APPLICABLE
The important shift is:
Don't just identify the incident. Identify the safety control that failed.
This is particularly important for HSSE because repeated barrier failures can reveal systemic weaknesses.
